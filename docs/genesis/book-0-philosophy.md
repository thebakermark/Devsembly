# Book 0 — Philosophy

**Status:** Binding
**Version:** 0.1.0

## North Star

**Engineer organizations that learn faster than the world changes.**

Devsembly exists to turn intent, knowledge, capability, evidence, and governed action
into organizations that can improve without losing human purpose or control.

## Foundational principles

### Organizations are systems

An organization is a living system of people, agents, knowledge, authority, policy,
capital, tools, processes, and feedback. Local optimization that harms the whole is not
success. Architecture must expose relationships, constraints, and consequences.

### Knowledge is a primary asset

Knowledge is not exhaust from completed work. It is a governed asset with provenance,
ownership, lifecycle, confidence, and access rules. Decisions, evidence, failures, and
lessons must remain usable beyond the session that produced them.

### Humans define intent and retain final authority

Humans establish purpose, acceptable risk, budgets, policy, and delegation. Automation
may prepare, recommend, execute, and verify within delegated bounds. It does not inherit
authority merely because it can act.

### Capability precedes implementation

Devsembly first defines what outcome and contract are required, then selects or builds
an implementation. A product, vendor, model, or tool is never the capability itself.

### Providers remain replaceable

External systems are providers behind explicit contracts. Provider selection considers
fitness, security, evidence, exit cost, and budget. Switching providers must not require
redesign of unrelated business rules.

### Memory is infrastructure

Reliable organizational memory requires identity, policy, retention, provenance,
retrieval, correction, and deletion controls. Agent context windows and chat history are
caches and inputs, not authoritative memory systems.

### Decisions require provenance

A material decision must preserve its context, alternatives, authority, evidence,
assumptions, expected cost, risk, and outcome. A conclusion without traceable provenance
cannot govern high-impact work.

### Architecture evolves deliberately

Architecture is changed through evidence, explicit tradeoffs, compatibility analysis,
and recorded decisions. Novelty and scale forecasts do not justify complexity by
themselves. Reversible decisions should remain reversible.

### Automation must increase understanding

Automation is successful when it improves speed, quality, safety, and organizational
understanding. Opaque automation that removes the ability to explain, inspect, stop,
repair, or learn is incomplete.

### Organizations continuously evolve

Policies, capabilities, roles, and workflows are versioned because environments change.
Every material workflow needs feedback that can revise assumptions and improve future
performance without silently rewriting history.

### Intelligence is collaborative

People, agents, models, tools, and recorded knowledge contribute different strengths.
Important work benefits from separation of duties, independent challenge, and evidence
rather than confidence or status.

### Engineering carries responsibility

Engineering choices allocate risk, access, time, cost, and power. Builders are
responsible for predictable misuse, operational burden, security boundaries, and the
human consequences of systems they create.

## Practical philosophical test

Before adopting a decision, ask:

1. Does it advance the North Star?
2. Does it preserve human purpose and final authority?
3. Does it strengthen knowledge, provenance, and learning?
4. Does it describe a capability before committing to a provider?
5. Does it remain understandable, inspectable, and stoppable?
6. Does it introduce complexity proportional to demonstrated need?
7. Does it make responsibility and consequences explicit?

A decision that fails this test requires revision, exception approval, or rejection.
